# Inconsistent-Travel-Time-Estimates-in-Time-Window-Vehicle-Routing: A Data-Quality Diagnosis from Last-Mile Delivery

Vehicle routing with time windows often treats travel-time uncertainty as if the uncertainty model itself were settled. Elicited three-point travel-time estimates are often internally inconsistent, and no ground truth confirms which repair rule is correct. This paper treats that inconsistency as a data-quality problem and measures it directly. On nine days of real pharmaceutical last-mile delivery data from the Attica region of Greece, the raw optimistic estimate exceeds the raw most-likely estimate on up to 22.89% of location pairs, and three repair rules disagree with each other by more than a minute on up to 15.13% of pairs on the most affected day. To test whether this ambiguity matters downstream, the paper uses leave-one-day-out cross-validation to check whether a uniformly tuned safety margin improves on-time delivery over no margin, and whether that conclusion survives being re-scored under all three repair rules. The margin raises the on-time rate by 2.63 percentage points (p = .004, n = 9 days), at a distance cost not statistically distinguishable from zero. The conclusion holds at a similar effect size (2.82 percentage points, p = .004) under all three repair rules: the ambiguity does not change which routing decision looks better here, even though the disagreement between rules is itself real and substantial. Robustness checks confirm neither result is an estimation artifact. The contribution is the diagnosis itself, and a demonstration of how to test, rather than assume, whether such ambiguity matters downstream.
